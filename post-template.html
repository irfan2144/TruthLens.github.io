What the Media Isn't Telling You About Sudan

Nearly 9 million people have been displaced. Cities lie in ruins. And the world's cameras are pointed elsewhere.

By TruthLens Editorial March 22, 2026 8 min read

Since April 2023, Sudan has been engulfed in a brutal civil war between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF). The death toll is in the tens of thousands. The number of displaced is among the highest recorded in any active conflict in recent history. And yet, compared to other conflicts, global media coverage has been strikingly sparse.

The Scale of What's Happening

According to the UN, over 8.8 million people have been internally displaced — the largest internal displacement crisis on Earth. Another 1.5 million have fled to neighbouring countries including Chad, Egypt, and South Sudan. Cities like Khartoum and El Fasher have experienced systematic destruction of hospitals, markets, and civilian infrastructure.

⚑ Verified Facts

· UN OCHA reports 25+ million people in need of humanitarian aid
· At least 15,000 civilians killed (independent estimates far higher)
· Famine declared in North Darfur, June 2024
· 70%+ of hospitals in conflict zones non-functional

Why Isn't This Front-Page News?

Part of the answer lies in geography and geopolitics. Sudan lacks the same level of Western entanglement as Ukraine, and the conflict involves no direct NATO interests. Media resources are finite, and editors make choices — conscious or not — about which wars are "relatable" to their audiences.

There is also the issue of access. Foreign journalists face extreme danger covering the Sudan conflict. RSF fighters have been accused of targeting journalists. Without boots on the ground, editors rely on wire reports, which reduce a complex humanitarian catastrophe into brief dispatch summaries buried on page 12.

"The people of Sudan are dying in silence — not because the world doesn't care, but because it has never been told to."

What You Can Do

Awareness is the first step. Share verified information from credible sources. Support organizations with direct operations in Sudan: MSF (Doctors Without Borders), UNHCR, and local NGOs coordinating food and medical aid. And when you see news coverage that ignores Sudan while covering less deadly conflicts at length — ask why.

TruthLens will continue to report on Sudan with the depth it deserves. The silence around this crisis is not neutral — it is a choice. And choices can be changed.
